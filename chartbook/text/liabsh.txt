Liabilities vary \textbf{by sector} and vary over time within sectors. Households and nonprofits (see {\color{orange!90!red}\textbf{---}}) have \$21.4 trillion in debt in 2026 Q2, equivalent to 65.8 percent of GDP. During the collapse of the housing bubble, in 2010, household and nonprofit debt was equivalent to 92.1 percent of GDP.

In 2026 Q2, private nonfinancial businesses (see {\color{green!72!black}\textbf{---}}), corporate and noncorporate, have total liabilities of \$45.7 trillion and debt of \$24.0 trillion. In 2026 Q2, nonfinancial business debt is equivalent to 73.8 percent of GDP, slightly below the pre-COVID ratio of 78.2 percent. Nonfinancial corporations have \$31.7 trillion in total liabilities and \$15.7 trillion in debt. 

Federal government debt (see {\color{blue!70!white}\textbf{---}}) is equivalent to 107.5 percent of GDP in the latest data, substantially above the pre-COVID ratio of 86.8 percent. State and local government debt (see {\color{paleblue}\textbf{---}}) is equivalent to 11.8 percent of GDP in 2026 Q2, slightly below 21.4 percent of GDP in 2010. Total liabilities for the sector, which include pensions, are 23.5 percent of GDP in the latest data, substantially below 44.5 percent in 2010.
