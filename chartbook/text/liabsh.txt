Liabilities vary \textbf{by sector} and vary over time within sectors. Households and nonprofits (see {\color{orange!90!red}\textbf{---}}) have \$21.1 trillion in debt in 2026 Q1, equivalent to 66.2 percent of GDP. During the collapse of the housing bubble, in 2010, household and nonprofit debt was equivalent to 92.1 percent of GDP.

In 2026 Q1, private nonfinancial businesses (see {\color{green!72!black}\textbf{---}}), corporate and noncorporate, have total liabilities of \$45.6 trillion and debt of \$22.6 trillion. In 2026 Q1, nonfinancial business debt is equivalent to 71.0 percent of GDP, slightly below the pre-COVID ratio of 76.9 percent. Nonfinancial corporations have \$31.9 trillion in total liabilities and \$14.5 trillion in debt. 

Federal government debt (see {\color{blue!70!white}\textbf{---}}) is equivalent to 108.3 percent of GDP in the latest data, substantially above the pre-COVID ratio of 86.8 percent. State and local government debt (see {\color{paleblue}\textbf{---}}) is equivalent to 11.7 percent of GDP in 2026 Q1, slightly below 21.4 percent of GDP in 2010. Total liabilities for the sector, which include pensions, are 24.8 percent of GDP in the latest data, substantially below 44.5 percent in 2010.
