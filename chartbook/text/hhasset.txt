According to the US Financial Accounts produced by the Federal Reserve, the market value of \textbf{household and nonprofit assets} is \$204.5 trillion in 2026 Q1, equivalent to 643 percent--or 6.43 years--of GDP. Of this, \$62.9 trillion, or 30.8 percent of the total, are tangible (non-financial) assets and \$141.6 trillion, or 69.2 percent, are financial assets.

Tangible assets include peoples' homes as well as consumer durable goods, such as cars, furniture, and appliances. Owner-occupied real estate is valued at \$48.7 trillion in 2026 Q1, equal to 153 percent of GDP \seebox{tealgreen!80!black}. The replacement value of consumer durable goods is \$8.9 trillion, or 28 percent of GDP.

Financial assets include equity in businesses--corporate and non-corporate--with a market value of \$81.4 trillion, or 256 percent of GDP \seebox{steelblue!90!black}, in 2026 Q1. Debt securities and loan assets total \$13.5 trillion, or 42 percent of GDP \seebox{blue!55!cyan}. Cash and deposits, including money market accounts, total \$20.6 trillion, or 65 percent of GDP \seebox{cyan!40!white}. Other financial assets total \$26.2 trillion.
