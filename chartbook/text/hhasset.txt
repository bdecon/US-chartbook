According to the US Financial Accounts produced by the Federal Reserve, the market value of \textbf{household and nonprofit assets} is \$197.3 trillion in 2025 Q2, equivalent to 647 percent--or 6.47 years--of GDP. Of this, \$62.7 trillion, or 31.8 percent of the total, are tangible (non-financial) assets and \$134.6 trillion, or 68.2 percent, are financial assets.

Tangible assets include peoples' homes as well as consumer durable goods, such as cars, furniture, and appliances. Owner-occupied real estate is valued at \$49.3 trillion in 2025 Q2, equal to 162 percent of GDP \seebox{green!60!teal}. The replacement value of consumer durable goods is \$8.5 trillion, or 28 percent of GDP.

Financial assets include equity in businesses--corporate and non-corporate--with a market value of \$76.6 trillion, or 251 percent of GDP \seebox{blue!65!black}, in 2025 Q2. Debt securities and loan assets total \$12.7 trillion, or 42 percent of GDP \seebox{blue!55!cyan}. Cash and deposits, including money market accounts, total \$19.7 trillion, or 65 percent of GDP \seebox{cyan!40!white}. Other financial assets total \$25.5 trillion.
