According to the US Financial Accounts produced by the Federal Reserve, the market value of \textbf{household and nonprofit assets} is \$217.8 trillion in 2026 Q2, equivalent to 670 percent--or 6.7 years--of GDP. Of this, \$64.3 trillion, or 29.5 percent of the total, are tangible (non-financial) assets and \$153.5 trillion, or 70.5 percent, are financial assets.

Tangible assets include peoples' homes as well as consumer durable goods, such as cars, furniture, and appliances. Owner-occupied real estate is valued at \$49.8 trillion in 2026 Q2, equal to 153 percent of GDP \seebox{tealgreen!80!black}. The replacement value of consumer durable goods is \$9.1 trillion, or 28 percent of GDP.

Financial assets include equity in businesses--corporate and non-corporate--with a market value of \$90.8 trillion, or 279 percent of GDP \seebox{steelblue!90!black}, in 2026 Q2. Debt securities and loan assets total \$13.1 trillion, or 40 percent of GDP \seebox{blue!55!cyan}. Cash and deposits, including money market accounts, total \$20.3 trillion, or 62 percent of GDP \seebox{cyan!40!white}. Other financial assets total \$29.3 trillion.
