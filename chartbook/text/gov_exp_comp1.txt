Net interest expense, the cost of federal borrowing, fell along with long-term interest rates, to 8.4 percent of outlays in 2019 from 14.8 percent in 1989 \seebox{orange!80!yellow}. 

 Offsetting reduced disceretionary spending, Medicare and Social Security now make up a larger share of federal spending, as a larger share of people are retirement age. Likewise, spending on the social safety net (means-tested benefits and Medicaid) increased as employment-to-population ratios fell and Medicaid was expanded. Medicare \seebox{blue!65!black}, Social Security \seebox{cyan!50!blue!95!white}, and the social safety net \seebox{cyan!95!white} combine to comprise 54.8 percent of federal spending in 2019, compared to 34.7 percent in 1989.
