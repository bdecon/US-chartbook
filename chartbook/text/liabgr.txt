Since the fourth quarter of 2019, inflation-adjusted US debt has increased at an annualized rate of 3.1 percent, slightly below the long-term rate of 3.7 percent. Over this five-year period, growth is driven largely by an increase in federal government debt. Federal government debt contributed 2.5 percentage points to annualized growth, and nonfinancial business debt contributed 0.4 percentage point.

Over the year ending 2024 Q4, real debt increased 1.6 percent, far below the long-term average. Federal government borrowing (see\cbox{blue!70!white}) contributed 1.9 percentage points to the overall change, while the state and local government did not contribute (see\cbox{cyan!60!white}). Households and nonprofits subtracted 0.2 percentage point (see\cbox{orange!90!red}), and nonfinancial businesses did not contribute (see\cbox{green!72!black}).
