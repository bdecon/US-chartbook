Since the second quarter of 2020, inflation-adjusted US debt increased at an annualized rate of 1.6 percent, far below the long-term rate of 3.7 percent. Over this six-year period, debt growth is driven largely by an increase in federal government debt. Federal government debt contributed 1.3 percentage points to annualized growth, and nonfinancial business debt contributed 0.2 percentage point.

Over the year ending 2026 Q2, real debt increased 2.5 percent, substantially below the long-term average. Federal government borrowing \seebox{blue!70!white} contributed 2.1 percentage points to the overall change, while state and local governments contributed 0.1 percentage point \seebox{paleblue}. Households and nonprofits did not contribute \seebox{orange!90!red}, and nonfinancial businesses contributed 0.4 percentage point \seebox{green!72!black}.
