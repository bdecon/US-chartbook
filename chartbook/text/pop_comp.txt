Population growth comes from two sources: natural increases and net migration. Natural increases are calculated as the number of births minus the number of deaths. Net migration is the number of people moving to the US (immigrants) minus the number of people moving out of the US (emigrants).

In the latest estimate, the US added 1.8 million people over the year ending July 2025, a population growth rate of 0.5 percent. There were 3.6 million births (see {\color{green!60!darkblue}\textbf{---}}), and 3.1 million deaths (see {\color{orange!70!darkred}\textbf{---}}), resulting in a natural increase of 520,000 people (see \begin{tikzpicture}\draw [pattern=crosshatch, pattern color=cyan!80!blue!50!white, draw=cyan!80!blue!50!white](0,0) rectangle (.2,.2); \end{tikzpicture}). In the same period, net migration from abroad increased the resident population by 1.3 million people \seebox{blue!75!darkgray}. For comparison, in 1989, there were 3.9 million births, 2.2 million deaths, and 580,000 net migrants to the US.
